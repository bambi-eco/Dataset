# Geometric error budget of the BAMBI projections: lens distortion, pose timing and pose correction

Material for the PERYTON paper (Section "Geometrical Preprocessing"). Three
steps of the pipeline that turns a frame into a georeferenced view are taken
out one at a time, and the ground position of every annotated animal is
recomputed without them:

- **lens undistortion**: the released 1024 x 1024 frames are undistorted with
  the calibration of the raw dataset version. Without it, the ray through a
  pixel leaves the camera in a slightly wrong direction, worst at the frame
  border.
- **pose timing from the SRT**: the published poses take their positions from
  the AirData flight log and their frame times from the DJI SRT subtitle file,
  with one clock offset fitted per recording. Without the SRT, frames have to
  be timed from the log's `isVideo` flag; without the log, positions come
  from the SRT's coarse coordinates.
- **pose correction**: the per-flight camera correction of Praschl et al.
  (IET Computer Vision 2026, doi 10.1049/cvi2.70077), an altitude offset and a
  heading offset per flight and per frame range (`<id>_correction.json`).

All three are compared with the point-matching radius of the detection
protocol (1.37 m, i.e. 20 px at 1024 px and 40 px at 2048 px), because an
error of that size turns a correct detection into a miss and a false positive.

All numbers in `results.md` are generated by the scripts below; the paper-ready
summary with the interpretation is in the sections after *Method*.

## Method

### Data

Base layer (poses, boxes, masks, corrections, metadata) of all flights of the
release, and the raw layer (SRT files, `air_data.csv`, `T_calib.json`,
`W_calib.json`) of every flight whose raw part is public on Zenodo. Both are
fetched with range requests (`download_from_zenodo.py --annotations-only`,
masks included), so no video is downloaded.

Height above ground per frame is referenced to the take-off point and is
independent of both the published altitude and the pose correction: the BEV
1 m terrain model at the take-off point, plus the flight log's barometric
height above take-off, minus the terrain at the nadir point
(`terrain.py --takeoff`, tiles read by window over HTTP). The corrected
camera height over the terrain model was used first and rejected, because in
36 flights the correction raises the camera by 44 to 52 m (see *Results:
pose correction*), which would have inflated every height-dependent error of
those flights. The flight log's own terrain column was rejected as well: it
disagrees with the DEM by 10 m and more (barometric altitude against a coarse
global terrain model).

All rows of the error budget are evaluated on one common set of boxes: frames
whose published pose is reproduced from the flight's own logs (`timing.py`)
and whose height lies between 5 and 250 m. Heights outside that range mark
poses that do not belong to the frame (the mis-placed recordings of *Dataset
issues*).

### Lens distortion (`distortion.py`)

1. The calibrations are grouped by their values; every distinct thermal and
   RGB calibration is one row of the inventory table, with the number of
   flights and drones it serves and the displacement of a raw pixel from its
   ideal pinhole position at the middle of the long edge and at the corner.
2. The new camera matrix of the released frames (focal length and principal
   point of the undistorted 1024 x 1024 image) is not stored anywhere. It is
   recovered from the validity mask shipped with every flight: the mask is the
   footprint of the raw frame under the undistortion map, and a coordinate
   descent over focal length and principal point reproduces it to a fraction
   of a percent of its pixels. This gives the frames' actual field of view and
   the exact raw position of every annotated pixel.
3. For every annotated thermal box the centre is mapped back into the raw
   frame through the lens model. Two rays are compared: through the
   undistorted pixel (pipeline) and through the raw pixel treated as a pinhole
   pixel (undistortion skipped). For a nadir camera over flat ground the
   ground offset between them is `h * |n_raw - n_undistorted|` with `h` the
   height above ground and `n` the normalized image coordinates; the camera
   tilt of the dataset is below one degree, so the flat nadir model is exact
   to that order.
4. Because the offset always points toward the nadir, it is systematic per
   image position and does not average out along a track. For every track
   the spread of the offset vectors (RMS radius and extent) is the apparent
   motion a tracker or a light-field integral would see.
5. As a side item, the scale error of assuming a 50 degree vertical field of
   view when the frames' own field of view differs is computed per box.

### Pose timing (`timing.py`)

For every recording (DJI splits long videos into several files; files inside
one `isVideo` run of the log are joined) the extractor is reproduced: the
AirData positions of the `isVideo` run, the SRT frame times, one clock offset
fitted by least squares on the two GPS traces. The reproduction matches the
published poses to a few centimetres, so the measured alternatives are
against the pipeline's own output:

- **AirData only**: frames timed from the `isVideo` onset at 30 fps (and with
  the recording's true mean frame rate);
- **SRT only**: the SRT coordinates at the fitted frame times;
- **log interpolation**: the log thinned to 1 Hz, error on the dropped rows,
  straight flight against turns.

Per-frame errors are looked up at every annotated box, so the distributions
are weighted like the detection benchmark. Side quantities: the RGB-thermal
frame pairing time difference from the two SRT files, and the disagreement of
the SRT gimbal yaw with the log's gimbal heading.

### Pose correction (`correction.py`)

Every correction file of the release carries two parameters, as a flight
default and, in 224 flights, per frame range (`fine_corrections`, first
matching range wins): an altitude offset (translation z, added to the camera
height) and a heading offset (rotation z, radians, subtracted from the
heading). The other four components are zero in every file. Per box, the
ground position is computed with the corrected and with the published pose for
a nadir camera over a plane at the take-off-referenced height, through the
undistorted pixel; the displacement is split into the altitude and the
heading part.

Both parameters are compared with quantities measured independently in the
raw logs, per flight over the annotated frames:

- altitude: DJI's take-off altitude (altitude above sea level minus height
  above take-off) against the terrain model at the take-off point, plus the
  scale term `h * (f_nominal / f_true - 1)` that a correction fitted in a
  renderer with the default 50 deg field of view absorbs;
- heading: the published heading, which is the aircraft's compass heading
  from the flight log, against the gimbal yaw in the SRT, i.e. the direction
  the camera faced.

### Reproducing

```bash
python download_from_zenodo.py --annotations-only -o data/base/<id>            # per flight
python download_from_zenodo.py --version raw --annotations-only -o data/raw/<id>
python evaluation/error_budget/terrain.py   --data data                        # terrain under every corrected nadir
python evaluation/error_budget/terrain.py   --data data --takeoff              # take-off-referenced heights
python evaluation/error_budget/distortion.py --data data --out evaluation/error_budget
python evaluation/error_budget/timing.py     --data data --out evaluation/error_budget
python evaluation/error_budget/correction.py --data data --out evaluation/error_budget
python evaluation/error_budget/report.py     --out evaluation/error_budget      # figures, LaTeX tables, results.md
```

## Results: lens distortion

**The lenses.** Four thermal and five RGB calibrations serve the whole dataset
(`tables/calibrations.csv`, `figures/lens_profile`). All thermal lenses have strong
barrel distortion (k1 between −0.34 and −0.39): the raw pixel sits 5.6 to
6.0 % closer to the optical centre than its ideal pinhole position at the
middle of the long edge and 8.3 to 9.2 % closer at the corner. The RGB
lenses are mild and mixed (k1 = +0.14, k2 = −0.26; at most 1.7 % outward,
0.6 to 0.8 % inward at the corner).

**The undistortion the release used.** The masks pin the new camera matrix of
the released frames down to within 0.01 % of their pixels. For 300 of the 301
thermal flights the focal length is 1124 to 1126 px at 1024 px, a vertical
field of view of 48.9 to 49.0 deg, and the same for 266 of 301 RGB flights.
The remaining 35 RGB flights (the Matrice 30T flights with 1080p RGB video,
drone instances 7 and 8) were undistorted to 36.5 deg, and one thermal
flight to 36.0 deg. The 154 flights whose poses are in the alfs_py format
store `fovy = 48.89`, consistent with the recovered value. The projection
code's default of 50 deg is therefore 2.3 to 2.5 % too wide; on the annotated
animals that is a median 0.32 m (p95 0.63 m) toward the frame edge, 1 % of the
boxes beyond the matching radius. 4.3 % of the thermal box centres lie in
the invalid border of the undistorted frame.

**What skipping the undistortion would do** (`figures/boxes_by_height`,
`tables/distortion_by_altitude.tex`). Over the 90,164 boxes the ground
offset has a median of 0.51 m, a mean of 0.69 m and a 95th percentile of
1.98 m; 41 % of the boxes move by more than half the matching radius and
15 % by more than the full radius. The offset is a product of height and
image position: it grows from a median of 0.25 m below 30 m to 0.87 m above
62 m (where 33 % of the boxes exceed the radius), and from 0.01 m in the inner
quarter of the frame to 0.93 m in the outer quarter and 1.78 m in the
corners (14 % of the boxes, 86 % of which exceed the radius). At the frame
edge the offset is 3.0 cm per metre of height. 45 % of all boxes lie in the
outer quarter of the frame (`figures/lens_density`).

**The offset is systematic, not noise.** It always points toward the nadir
and depends on where in the frame the animal is, so it does not average out
along a track: the offset vectors of one track have a median RMS radius of
0.45 m (p95 1.03 m) and a median extent of 1.68 m (p95 4.3 m); 61 % of the
4,954 tracks would appear to move by more than the matching radius without
having moved. Two frames that see the same animal at different image positions
therefore disagree, which is the case that matters for georeferenced tracking
and for the light-field integral, where every view would place the animal at
its own offset.

## Results: pose timing

**What the logs look like** (`tables/timing_recordings.tex`, `figures/onset_lag`,
`figures/onset_cost`). AirData logs at 5 Hz (201 recordings) or 10 Hz (99); 31
recordings have a gap longer than 2 s (up to 384 s), where the extractor falls
back to the SRT. The SRT frame times run at 29.98 fps (29.65 to 30.05). The
fitted SRT-to-AirData clock offset has a median of 0.61 s and ranges from
−23 to +30 s, so it has to be fitted per recording. Drone speed is 3.0 m/s
median, 5.0 m/s at the 95th percentile.

**Timing from the flight log alone.** The `isVideo` flag comes on after the
first frame in 95 % of the recordings, by a median 0.51 s and up to 1.03 s at
the 95th percentile (57 % of the recordings between 0.3 and 0.9 s; the
extremes are −282 s, a log whose video run begins long before the first frame,
and +4.9 s). At the annotated animals this costs a median
1.54 m (mean 2.51 m, p95 6.2 m): 55 % of the boxes move beyond the matching
radius and 23 % by more than 4 m. Using the recording's true frame rate
instead of 30 fps does not help (median 1.78 m), because the onset lag, not
the frame rate, is the error. The lag varies between recordings, so it cannot
be calibrated away.

**Positions from the SRT alone.** With the clock offset known, the SRT
coordinates are within 0.13 m (median) and 0.66 m (p95) of the published
poses; only 2 % of the boxes move beyond the matching radius. But 14 of the
300 recordings carry stale coordinates in the SRT, off by 5 to 3,100 m for
the whole recording, so the SRT cannot be the only source either.

**Interpolating the log.** Thinned to 1 Hz, the AirData track interpolates
to 0.07 m (p95) on straight legs and 0.30 m (p95) in turns, median over
recordings; the native 5 to 10 Hz is more than enough.

**Side quantities.** The thermal and RGB SRT files pair frames within
7 ms (median) and 9 ms (p95) on the camera clock, i.e. 2 to 5 cm of drone
motion, so the residual RGB-thermal offset of the orthographic views is not a
frame-pairing effect. The SRT gimbal yaw and the log's gimbal heading disagree
by a median 6.2 deg and up to 21 deg (p95), a constant per recording; at the
frame edge (20 m from nadir at 45 m height) 6 deg is 2 m, which is what the
per-flight rotation correction of the dataset absorbs.

## Results: pose correction

**What the corrections contain** (`tables/correction_flights.csv`). 231 of the
301 flights carry an altitude correction on their annotated frames and 224 a
heading correction; 224 flights use frame-range corrections (343 ranges, one
of them with its end before its start, so it never applies). The altitude
offsets have a median magnitude of 4.6 m (signed median −3.4 m, 80 %
negative, range −17 to +55 m), the heading offsets 4.5 deg (range −30 to
+9 deg). 6.4 % of the boxes lie on frames without any correction.

**What omitting them would do** (`figures/correction_boxes`). Over the common
set the animals move by a median of 1.23 m (mean 2.31 m, p95 7.4 m); 71 % of
the boxes move by more than half the matching radius and 45 % by more than
the full radius. The altitude offset alone moves them by a median of 1.01 m
(33 % beyond the radius), the heading offset alone by 0.24 m (23 % beyond
the radius: small for most flights, 4.1 m in median where the heading offset
exceeds 5 deg). The effect is largest at 52 to 62 m (median 3.7 m, 75 %
beyond the radius), where the Feldbach flights (below) dominate; without them
the median is 1.19 m and 43 % of the boxes exceed the radius.

**The corrections are physically interpretable** (`figures/correction_heading`,
`figures/correction_altitude`).

- Heading: the published heading is exactly the aircraft's compass heading of
  the flight log. The heading correction reproduces the difference between
  that compass heading and the camera's gimbal yaw in the SRT with r = 0.92,
  within 2 deg for 62 % and within 5 deg for 91 % of the 223 flights with
  both. The flights split into two groups, with a compass-minus-gimbal
  difference near 0 or near −17 deg, and the correction follows both.
- Altitude: outside 37 flights (below), the altitude correction equals DJI's
  take-off altitude error against the terrain model plus the field-of-view
  term within 1 m for 58 % and within 2 m for 87 % of the 194 flights (median
  absolute residual 0.87 m). Without the field-of-view term the median
  residual is −0.84 m, with it +0.24 m: the corrections contain the 2.3 %
  scale of the 50 deg default against the frames' 49 deg.
- The 63 flights without any correction on their annotated frames have a
  median compass-minus-gimbal difference of 0.5 deg and a take-off altitude
  error of −0.1 m; they did not need one.

**36 Feldbach flights with corrections of 44 to 52 m.** In 36 flights of the
Feldbach campaigns (October and November 2024, both drone models) the
altitude correction exceeds the take-off reference by 44 to 52 m (median
47 m), nearly constant within the campaign, while the published altitude
itself agrees with the take-off terrain. The size matches the geoid
undulation in Austria, which points to a vertical-datum mismatch between the
poses and the terrain model these flights were rendered with, absorbed by the
correction. This should be checked against that terrain model. One further
flight shows a residual of 136 m, which is a take-off reference failure.

## Error budget

`tables/error_budget.tex`; all rows on the common set of 90,164 thermal boxes.

| effect | median | mean | p95 | > 0.69 m | > 1.37 m |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| lens distortion ignored | 0.51 m | 0.69 m | 1.98 m | 41 % | 15 % |
| distortion, apparent motion along a track (RMS, 4,954 tracks) | 0.45 m | 0.49 m | 1.03 m | 22 % | 1 % |
| frame times from the flight log alone | 1.54 m | 2.51 m | 6.24 m | 76 % | 55 % |
| positions from the SRT alone | 0.13 m | 1.70 m | 0.66 m | 5 % | 2 % |
| pose correction omitted | 1.23 m | 2.31 m | 7.35 m | 71 % | 45 % |
| altitude offset only | 1.01 m | 1.57 m | 4.38 m | 66 % | 33 % |
| heading offset only | 0.24 m | 1.15 m | 5.69 m | 27 % | 23 % |
| 50 deg field of view instead of 49 deg | 0.33 m | 0.34 m | 0.60 m | 2 % | 0 % |

All three steps are of the order of the matching radius: timing from the log
alone would move most animals beyond it, the pose correction almost half,
lens distortion one in seven. The errors are independent (speed and flag
delay; height and image position; per-flight altitude and heading offsets)
and add in quadrature.

## Dataset issues found on the way

**Poses of later recordings in multi-recording flights.** In 13 flights with
several video files (17, 35, 57, 59, 60, 119, 132, 156, 158, 160, 229, 239,
243) the published poses of the second and later recordings do not lie on the
flight path: they form a straight line 28 to 1,300 m away from where both logs
put the drone, which looks like a linear extrapolation of the first
recording's log segment rather than a lookup in the recording's own `isVideo`
run (`tables/timing_recordings.csv`, verdict `unmatched`). 3,344 annotated
boxes (3 % of all) fall into these recordings: flight 158 (2,380 boxes), 156
(272), 243 (217), 119 (189), 35 (168), 160 (46), 132 (40), 239 (32). Flight
119 is the one the paper already drops for geometrically inconsistent
projected boxes. Several of these frames lie hundreds of metres below the
terrain. The fix is in the extractor: match each recording to its own
`isVideo` run of the log.

**Pose files of other flights.** The pose files of flights 4 and 6 are
byte-identical to that of flight 2, the one of flight 159 to that of 161, and
flight 15 carries the timestamps of flight 17; none of them matches the
flight's own logs (6,082 annotated boxes).

**Datum offset of the Feldbach campaigns.** See *Results: pose correction*.
